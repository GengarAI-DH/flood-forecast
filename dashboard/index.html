How the algorithm works

Any trend, in tech, money, culture, health or anything else, moves through the same six stages: it emerges, money floods in, it goes mainstream, how-tos and copycats boom, content floods social media, then it is crowded. A collector runs every 6 hours, watching key people in world power, money, AI and industry, plus stocks, politics, world events, news and social buzz, across sectors from AI and jobs to farms, oil, electricity, water and infrastructure. The model learns how long each stage lasts and forecasts what is next. Hands-on fields buy about — extra years per barrier point, and every year later a trend starts shortens its runway by — years.

  1. Each trend gets a year for every stage it has reached.
  2. The model learns the runway (years from job spike to flooded) from two things: when the trend started, and how hard the field is to enter.
  3. Fields that still are not crowded, like wind turbine and EV techs, count as "at least this long", so hands-on work isn't underestimated.
  4. Every past trend is hidden one at a time and predicted, which gives the honest error range.

Stage years for past trends are estimates from well-documented history, not measured job-posting series. Treat forecasts as ranges, not dates. Surprises like a new law, a crash or a breakthrough can break any model.
